# Editorial and publication policy

Every page in this repository is public and can be shared by direct URL. This
makes early review by collaborators possible. Public availability alone does
not place a page in the reader-facing guide.

## Exact-version approval

The guide owner reviews an exact version of each Markdown file. Only an
approved file hash enters ordinary navigation, site search, and the sitemap.
If the file changes, it automatically returns to **changed since review**
until the new version is read.

The possible editorial states are:

- **unread** — public by direct URL, not yet read by the owner;
- **needs revision** — public by direct URL, with revision requested;
- **approved** — this exact version is listed and indexed;
- **changed since review** — a previously approved page has changed.

This is editorial review, not peer review or a mathematical correctness badge.
The [review workspace](../review/index.md) is itself reachable only by direct
URL and never enters the listed guide.

## Page selection

Every page must have a plausible reader and use. The site does not publish
autogenerated claim pages, placeholder stubs, internal task packets, or a page
for every technical lemma. Major-result pages are selective; external
attribution is inclusive through the developments ledger.

## Expository shape

The mathematics determines the structure of a page. A conceptual essay may
begin with a toy example; a research essay may begin with a specimen problem;
a classification theorem may begin with the invariant that makes the answer
possible. Headings should name those mathematical objects and transitions.

A major-result page still owes the reader several things:

- a precise statement with its actual hypotheses;
- an explanation of the mechanism or proof architecture;
- enough interpretation to say why the result changes the landscape;
- any limitation needed to understand the mathematical claim.

These obligations do not require a universal sequence of headings. Proof
links, formal-check scope, publication status, chronology, and detailed
attribution normally live in the [proof and evidence
ledger](../results/evidence-ledger.md). The reading page can then end with the
mathematical consequence or the next obstruction.

A result's inclusion means that the guide accepts responsibility for an
accurate account of the cited claim. The linked paper, manuscript,
formalization, or certificate remains the proof-bearing object.
