# About this guide

**The Jacobian Conjecture: A Guide** is a public account of the 2026
counterexample, the surviving plane problem, and the mathematics that has
formed around them. The guide is edited and primarily written by **Nathaniel
Monson**, with project-wide AI assistance disclosed separately. It is written
for research mathematicians entering from neighboring fields and for graduate
students who want a reliable route into the new landscape.

The central expository aim is simple: a reader should see why each idea is
needed before meeting its full technical machinery. The start path moves from
the explicit formula to the marked-root cover and then to the boundary at
infinity. The idea essays develop one tool at a time from a concrete question.
The research essays use those tools to formulate open problems precisely.

## Two layers with different jobs

The main pages are a **reading layer**. They explain the mechanism, identify
the surprising step, and connect one result to the next. Proof links,
publication status, detailed scope, and role-specific attribution live in the
[proof and evidence ledger](../results/evidence-ledger.md) and the broader
[external-developments ledger](../developments/index.md).

Mathematical qualifications that affect an argument remain in the exposition.
Archival qualifications remain available without interrupting every
conceptual paragraph.

## What belongs here

- introductions that make a technical source easier to enter;
- mathematical essays that connect formulas with geometric pictures;
- research narratives organized around concrete questions;
- selected result pages with precise statements and conceptual proof routes;
- ledgers that preserve sources, chronology, evidence, and credit.

Detailed proofs, computations, working claims, and research lanes belong in
the technical research repository or in pinned public manuscripts. A guide
page explains why a result matters and where its proof lives; it is not
usually the first publication venue for the theorem.

## Authorship and responsibility

The prose is human-directed and owner-edited rather than emitted from a claim
database. AI systems have assisted with exploration, drafting, copy-editing,
source discovery, code, testing, and adversarial review. Monson selects the
claims, controls the exposition and attribution, and is responsible for what
the guide publishes.
