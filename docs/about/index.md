# About this guide

**The Jacobian Conjecture: A Guide** is a public, hand-authored account of
the 2026 counterexample, the surviving plane problem, and the mathematics
that has developed around them. It is written for research mathematicians
outside the immediate subject and for graduate students who want a reliable
way into the new landscape.

The guide is edited and primarily written by **Nathaniel Monson**. It uses AI
assistance under the [project-wide disclosure](ai-assistance.md).

## What belongs here

- introductions that make a technical source easier to enter;
- mathematical-ideas essays with worked examples and reading routes;
- stories that connect several results into a research front;
- selective pages for major results, with precise statements and public proof
  routes;
- a comprehensive catalogue of relevant external developments known to us.

Every page should be something a reader might deliberately visit and use.
Mathematical prose is written and revised as a page, not generated from a
claim database.

## What belongs elsewhere

The private research-state repository owns detailed proofs, computations,
working claims, and technical lanes. This guide does not mirror it. It
selects only results that deserve public exposition and have a usable public
source route.

The guide is not normally the first publication venue for a theorem. A
result page explains and links; the cited paper, manuscript, formalization,
or certificate is where the mathematical claim and proof are supplied.

## What inclusion means

A result's appearance here does not mean that the guide's editors have
independently checked its proof. For external work, the guide reports what a
cited public source claims. For project work, the linked mathematical source
is where correctness is asserted.

The guide does take responsibility for accurate exposition: sources should be
described in their actual form, hypotheses and conclusions should not be
silently strengthened, and attribution should be corrected when better
public evidence appears.

## Two different kinds of completeness

Our own research is presented selectively. External work is tracked
inclusively within the guide's declared scope. A minor external contribution
may receive only one line in the developments ledger, but it should not be
left out because it is minor.
