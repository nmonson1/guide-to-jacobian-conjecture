# Attack on the open `(8,28)` Newton case

## Outcome

1. The two exact saturated sparse coefficient ideals for `[P,Q]=x^2` were generated over `Q`.
2. The complete upper-`x`-degree linearized Jacobian complexes were computed exactly, including bases for every kernel and cokernel.
3. The first nonlinear obstruction forces the first deformation to remain tangent to the common approximate-root locus.
4. The common toric `Kbar=-5` divisor forces a degree-21 Belyi map with passport
   `(2^10 1), (3^7), (17 1^4)`.
5. Therefore Borisov's Three-dessin framework, whose core `-5` map has degree 16, cannot realize either open Newton polygon.
6. The same divisor contributes `e f=21`, so any counterexample in this Newton case has generic degree at least 21.

## 1. Exact coefficient ideals

For a support pair `S_P,S_Q`, write

`P=sum p_ij x^i y^j`, `Q=sum q_kl x^k y^l`.

The coefficient of `x^a y^b` in `[P,Q]` is

`J_ab = sum_{i+k-1=a, j+l-1=b} (i l-j k) p_ij q_kl`.

The ideal is generated by

`J_ab-delta_(a,b),(2,0)`

and saturated by the product of coefficients at all polygon vertices.

## 2. Upper-face filtration

Write

`P=sum_{i=0}^8 x^i p_i(y)`, `Q=sum_{j=0}^{12}x^j q_j(y)`.

The coefficient of `x^19` gives

`8 p_8 q_12' - 12 p_8' q_12=0`.

The vertex conditions imply, after normalization,

`p_8=R^2`, `q_12=R^3`, `R=y^7(y-1)`.

Put `u_a=p_(8-a)`, `v_b=q_(12-b)`.  The coefficient of `x^(19-r)` is

`E_r=sum_{a+b=r} ((8-a)u_a v_b'-(12-b)u_a'v_b)`.

Except at `r=17`, it must vanish.  Along the lower support edge put

`u_a=y^(14-2a) U_a`, `v_b=y^(21-2b)V_b`.

The bilinear layer operator is

`B_ab(U,V)=(3a-2b)UV+(8-a)yUV'-(12-b)yU'V`.

The linearized operator at layer `r` is

`L_r(U_r,V_r)=B_(r,0)(U_r,(y-1)^3)+B_(0,r)((y-1)^2,V_r)`.

At `r=1`, every kernel vector has

`u_1=(2/3)y^12 A(y)`, `v_1=y^19(y-1)A(y)`,

with `deg A<=2` in the truncated case and `deg A<=3` in the full case.
The quadratic forcing at `r=2` pairs with the cokernel as

- truncated: `(14/3) A(1)^2`;
- full: `(22/3) A(1)^2` and `-(8/3)A(1)^2`.

Hence `A(1)=0`.  Writing `A=(y-1)B`, the deformation becomes

`u_1=2Rh`, `v_1=3R^2h`, `h=(1/3)y^5B`.

Thus the first nonlinear obstruction does not kill the case; it forces the first correction to be a change of approximate root.

## 3. Toric boundary and the forced degree-21 Belyi map

Use transformed coordinates `X,Y`, with the inverse Laurent substitution

`X=x^(-1)`, `Y=x^4 y`.

For a toric ray `(r,s)`, the source `Kbar` label is

`(-r)+(4r+s)=3r+s`.

A common smooth refinement of both normal fans has negative-label chain

`-1 -- -3 -- -5 -- -2`

with source rays `(0,-1),(-1,0),(-2,1),(-1,1)` and self-intersections `-2,-2,-1,-1`.

| label | `(v(P),v(Q))` | normal index | behavior |
|---:|---|---:|---|
|-1|`(-16,-24)=8(-2,-3)`|8|point on target `-5`|
|-3|`(-8,-12)=4(-2,-3)`|4|point on target `-5`|
|-5|`(-2,-3)`|1|dominates target `-5`|
|-2|`(-1,-1)`|1|degree 1 onto target `-2`|

On the `-5` divisor, `z=XY^2` is a coordinate.  The lower face has the form

`P_face=X p(z)`, `Q_face=X^2Y q(z)`,

where `deg p=7`, `deg q=10`, and all endpoint coefficients are nonzero.  Taking the lowest toric weight in `[P,Q]=X^2` gives

`p q+2z p q'-3z p' q=1`.

The target `-5` coordinate is

`tau=Q^2/P^3`,

and on the source divisor

`tau=z q^2/p^3`.

The face equation gives

`tau'=q/p^4`.

It follows that `p` and `q` are coprime and squarefree, and have no zero at `z=0`.  Hence `tau` has degree 21 and passport

- over `0`: `2^10 1`;
- over `infinity`: `3^7`;
- over `tau(infinity)`: `17 1^4`.

The Frobenius character formula gives `5*21!` ordered permutation triples for this passport.  All are transitive and have trivial automorphism group, so there are exactly five connected dessins up to isomorphism.

Borisov's Three-dessin framework uses the same core `-5` Belyi map as the First Framework, of degree 16.  Mapping degree of a divisorial valuation is unchanged by further blowups.  Therefore neither open Newton case can realize Three-dessin.

Finally, over the generic point of the target `-5` divisor,

`mu=sum e_i f_i`.

The forced divisor contributes `1*21`; therefore `mu>=21` for every counterexample in the `(8,28)` Newton case.

## 4. What remains

The two sparse ideals have not been proved inconsistent.  The main reduction now is finite:

1. construct the five degree-21 Belyi maps explicitly;
2. substitute each lower-face orbit into the full/truncated ideals;
3. quotient the upper-face common-root gauge;
4. solve the remaining weight layers, with exact saturation certificates.

The first unavoidable obstruction found so far is not at the linearized or first nonlinear layer.  It lies in global compatibility between the five forced lower-face dessins and the upper approximate-root expansion.
