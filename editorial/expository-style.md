# Expository style for the guide

This is a working editorial document, not a public mathematics page. Its aim
is to preserve a recognizable voice while leaving each author room to solve
the local expository problem.

## The desired voice

Controlled, exact, and conceptually ambitious. Increase the temperature only
slightly: enough to register surprise, beauty, and mathematical judgment,
without turning the guide into a performance.

A strong page lets the reader hear four judgments:

1. what is surprising;
2. which picture is useful;
3. where the real difficulty lies;
4. why the next idea is needed.

## Make the idea feel necessary

Whenever possible, use this progression:

1. pose a concrete question;
2. try the natural picture;
3. expose the obstruction;
4. introduce the concept that resolves it;
5. return to the counterexample or plane problem.

Toy examples should contain the mechanism, not merely decorate the page.

## Add causal glue

Spend extra words on implications, not ceremonial background. A compressed
claim should usually expose its mechanism:

```text
no finite ramification
    -> failure of properness
    -> an escaping sequence
    -> boundary geometry.
```

One extra sentence naming the intermediate object is often enough.

## Use authorial judgment

Prefer sentences such as:

- `The affine chart is the surprising step.`
- `The striking compression is from a coefficient problem to five dessins.`
- `A Puiseux expansion is a microscope for infinity.`
- `Normalization is where the coordinate formula gives way to an intrinsic
  finite cover.`

These are interpretive claims. They should be earned by the surrounding
mathematics and used sparingly.

## Avoid habitual antithesis

The central paradox deserves strong contrast. Secondary prose usually
improves when a negative contrast becomes a forward causal statement.

```text
A dessin does not prove globalization.
```

becomes

```text
A dessin determines the first boundary layer; globalization requires
compatible solutions at every later layer.
```

Retain `not A, but B` only when the contrast itself carries the idea.

## Remove metadiscourse

Replace subjects such as `this page`, `this front`, `this result`, and `the
concrete target` with the mathematical object or question. Use navigation
sentences only when they genuinely orient the reader.

## Let headings belong to the mathematics

Avoid universal templates such as:

- `What is true and why`
- `Precise result`
- `Discussion`
- `What it does not prove`
- `Proof source and status`

Use headings such as `The affine chart is the surprising step`, `How the
boundary recovers q`, or `The remaining globalization problem`. Link the
result page to the proof and evidence ledger for audit material.

## Separate reading from audit

The reading layer carries the mathematical argument and any limitation needed
to understand it. The ledger carries evidence type, exact proof source,
formal-check scope, chronology, attribution, and publication status.

A useful conversion is:

```text
X does not prove Y.
```

into

```text
X proves A. Reaching Y additionally requires B.
```

## Use heuristic freedom honestly

A short box titled `The picture`, `How to think about it`, or `Pause and
check` may simplify the situation deliberately. Follow it with the precise
statement when the simplification suppresses a hypothesis or exceptional
case.

Do not manufacture a discovery narrative. It is enough to say which viewpoint
makes the formula intelligible or which tempting approach fails and why.

## Diagrams are mathematical sentences

A diagram should reveal structure: several sheets over one target,
normalization plus deleted boundary, a loop permuting a fiber, a Newton edge,
or a dessin and its passport. Captions should state the mathematical point.

Keep visual conventions stable across pages. Never draw finite ramification
inside the affine source when the repeated-root point belongs to the deleted
boundary.

## End with mathematical motion

A page should normally end by resolving its opening question, naming the next
obstruction, or giving the reader a durable mental model. Sources and status
belong behind a ledger link, not as the final rhetorical event.
