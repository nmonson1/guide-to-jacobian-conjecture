# Incomplete planar proof strategy: terminal descent or complexity reduction

`RMU-6D8E0011` · `heuristic` · statement version `1`

## Exact statement

Incomplete proof strategy—not a proof of the planar Jacobian conjecture. Starting from a hypothetical minimal plane Keller counterexample, run an exhaustive normalized Newton queue to a terminal complete-chain system. The missing global theorem would show that every terminal system either fails simultaneous finite polynomial support in adjacent boundary charts or is induced by an admissible polynomial approximate-root operation that strictly lowers Newton complexity. Exact calculations rule out several direct descents of the displayed three-dimensional Keller and five-dimensional constant-Hessian examples, but they do not prove this global dichotomy or exhaust all possible descents.

## Hypotheses

- The starting reduction from a minimal plane Keller counterexample to the normalized support queue is valid and exhaustive.
- Every saturation complement, coefficient branch and adjacent rechart is represented in the queue.
- The chosen Newton complexity is well founded and strictly decreases under every operation counted as a descent.

## Applies to

- A proposed global proof architecture joining Lane 8 terminal Newton reduction to Lane 9 adjacent-chart descent.
- The explicitly encoded affine-plane, polynomial-graph, linear-target and six-parameter square-correction tests for the two displayed higher-dimensional examples.

## Limitations

- This unit is an incomplete proof strategy, not a theorem and not a proof of the planar Jacobian conjecture.
- The checked descent families are not known to exhaust all higher-dimensional descents.
- Queue exhaustiveness, adjacent-chart realization, two-sided finite-support exclusion and complexity reduction remain unproved.
- The literature attribution and source formulas require a separate review before publication.
