## Mathematical record

`RMU-C1DD871B` · `proposition`

At the geometric generic point of every prime divisor of \(Y\), the retained
and deleted sheets have one of the four types \(U0\), \(U1\), \(U2\), or
\(B\).  Here \(U0\) has three retained unramified sheets; \(U1\) and \(U2\)
have respectively one and two deleted unramified sheets; and \(B\) has a
retained unramified sheet and a deleted ramified pair.  In particular a
deleted three-cycle cannot occur.  The generic monodromy of the cubic
extension is \(S_3\).

- **proof:** A proof body follows this labelled manuscript statement. — Inline support is reproduced on the unit record.

If \(D=V(h)\) is a target prime divisor, then \(h\circ F\) is a nonconstant
polynomial on \(U\).  Every irreducible factor of \(h\circ F\) gives a
divisorial valuation of \(\C(U)\) lying over \(D\).  The Keller condition
makes the corresponding retained branch unramified.  Thus inertia in the
degree-three permutation representation fixes a retained sheet.  If inertia
is trivial, zero, one, or two of the three unramified sheets may be deleted,
giving \(U0\), \(U1\), or \(U2\).  If inertia is a transposition, its ramified
pair cannot be retained by the \'{e}tale map \(U\to Y\), so that pair is
deleted and the fixed unramified sheet is retained, giving \(B\).  These are
precisely the four stated patterns.

If the cubic extension were cyclic Galois, the retained unramified branch
would force trivial inertia over every target divisor.  The finite
normalization would be unramified in codimension one and hence, by purity,
finite étale over \(\A^3\).  Since complex affine space has no nontrivial
connected finite étale cover, the extension would be trivial, a
contradiction.
