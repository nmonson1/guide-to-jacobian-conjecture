# The Degree-Five and Degree-Six Conic Frontier

<p class="dek">After the fixed-factor exclusions, the conic frontier is concentrated in three explicit boundary problems: the binary cubic-factor quintic, quintic basepoint strata, and the primitive sextic conic.</p>

<span class="status status-kind">Open Problem</span> <span class="status coverage-complete">Exact manuscript location</span>

## Precise statement

Determine whether a Keller map can occur in any of the remaining conic strata: \(G(x,y)(x^2,xy,y^2)\) for a squarefree binary cubic \(G\); a degree-five fixed-factor model where every component of \(G=0\) sees constant \(A/B\); or a primitive degree-six conic leading image with no fixed factor.

### [JCG-44542689 · Basepoint-Free Fixed-Factor Conic Strata Are Excluded in Degrees Five and Six](../claims/JCG-44542689.md)

Under the stated invariant-gap and basepoint-free hypotheses, the fixed-factor conic leading-form patterns (g,e)=(1,2),(3,1) in degree five and (2,2),(4,1) in degree six cannot occur for a nonautomorphic Keller map.

### [JCG-B1FF96FC · The Genuinely Three-Variable Quintic Cubic-Factor Stratum Is Empty](../claims/JCG-B1FF96FC.md)

For a squarefree genuinely three-variable cubic G3, no Keller jet has highest form G3(x,y,z)(x^2,xy,y^2); the surviving cubic-fixed-factor case is the binary overlap.
