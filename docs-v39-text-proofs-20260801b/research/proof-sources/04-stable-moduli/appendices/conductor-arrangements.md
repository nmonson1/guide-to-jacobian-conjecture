\section{Exact conductors and growing stable moduli}
\label{app:conductor-arrangements}

We return to the cubic frame with
\[
h(T,C)=A(C)T^3+B(C)T^2.
\]
The target fiber cubic is
\[
P(T)=A(c)T^3+B(c)T^2+vT-2u,
\]
with discriminant
\[
\Delta_{A,B}
=B^2v^2-4Av^3+8B^3u-108A^2u^2-36ABuv.
\]
Assume first that \(\gcd(A,B)=1\).

\subsection{The conductor}

\begin{theorem}[Exact discriminant conductor]
\label{thm:cubic-frame-conductor}
The map
\[
\nu:\A^2_{c,t}\longrightarrow V(\Delta_{A,B}),
\]
\[
v=-3A(c)t^2-2B(c)t,\qquad
u=-A(c)t^3-\frac12B(c)t^2
\]
is the normalization.  If
\[
H(c,t)=3A(c)t+B(c),
\]
then the conductor in \(\C[c,t]\) is exactly
\[
H^2\C[c,t].
\]
Its contraction to the discriminant coordinate ring is generated by
\[
B^2-3Av,\qquad -18Au-Bv.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Irreducibility follows from
\[
\Disc_u(\Delta_{A,B})=64(B^2-3Av)^3,
\]
since \(B^2-3Av\) is not a square in \(\C(c,v)\).  B\'ezout coefficients
for \(A,B\) combine the equations
\[
3At^2+2Bt+v=0,\qquad Bt^2+2vt-6u=0
\]
into a monic quadratic equation for \(t\), proving finiteness.  The
identities
\[
B^2-3Av=H^2,\qquad
-18Au-Bv=2tH^2
\]
give birationality.  If
\[
R=\C[u,v,c]/(\Delta_{A,B}),\qquad S=\C[c,t],
\]
then the monic quadratic also gives \(S=R[t]=R+Rt\).  Since both
\(H^2\) and \(tH^2\) belong to \(R\), it follows that
\(H^2S\subset R\); hence \(H^2S\) lies in the conductor.

Set \(K=\C(c)\).  Because \(H=3At+B\), direct substitution gives
\[
v=\frac{B^2-H^2}{3A},\qquad
u=\frac{-B^3+3BH^2-2H^3}{54A^2}.
\]
The earlier identities also give \(H^2,tH^2\in R\), and hence
\(H^3=3AtH^2+BH^2\in R\otimes_{\C[c]}K\).  Thus the generic inclusion is
exactly
\[
K[H^2,H^3]\subset K[H],
\]
whose conductor is \(H^2K[H]\).  The polynomial \(H=3At+B\) is primitive
and irreducible because \(\gcd(A,B)=1\).  Therefore every global conductor
element has \(H\)-valuation at least two and is divisible by \(H^2\) in the
UFD \(S\).  This proves that the conductor is exactly \(H^2S\).

Finally, \(S=R+Rt\) shows that this conductor, viewed as an ideal of \(R\),
is generated by \(H^2\) and \(tH^2\).  The two displayed identities identify
those generators, up to the unit two, with
\(B^2-3Av\) and \(-18Au-Bv\).
\end{proof}

Thus the reduced conductor support
\[
L_{A,B}=V(3A(c)t+B(c))
\]
is intrinsic to the discriminant surface, not merely detected by a chosen
gradient calculation.

\subsection{Marked arrangements}

Assume \(A\) is squarefree and a nonempty subset of its roots marks planes
on which an infinity sheet is missing.  In the normalization these planes
pull back to vertical lines \(M_\rho=(c=\rho)\).

\begin{theorem}[Stable conductor-arrangement classification]
\label{thm:conductor-arrangement-classification}
Assume \(A,A'\) are squarefree and
\[
\gcd(A,B)=\gcd(A',B')=1.
\]
Two marked arrangements
\[
\left(\A^2,L_{A,B},\{M_\rho\}_{\rho\in S}\right)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\left(\A^2,L_{A',B'},\{M_{\rho'}\}_{\rho'\in S'}\right)
\]
become isomorphic after multiplication by an affine space if and only if
there exist
\[
\gamma(c)=\lambda c+\mu,\quad \lambda\ne0,\qquad
\nu,\kappa\in\C^*,\qquad h(c)\in\C[c],
\]
such that \(\gamma\) carries the marked roots to the marked roots and
\[
A'(\gamma(c))\nu=\kappa A(c),
\]
\[
B'(\gamma(c))+3A'(\gamma(c))h(c)=\kappa B(c).
\]
Stabilization introduces no additional transformations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If a cylinder automorphism has first coordinate \(C\), mapping one marked
line to another gives equality of principal prime ideals and hence
\[
C-\rho'=\lambda(c-\rho).
\]
Thus \(C=\lambda c+\mu\), independent of \(t\) and of all stabilization
variables.  The conductor curves are irreducible principal divisors, so
\[
3A'(C)T+B'(C)=\kappa(3A(c)t+B(c)).
\]
Solving this identity shows that \(T\) is independent of all stabilization
variables and has the form \(T=\nu(c)t+h(c)\).  In the full cylinder
Jacobian, the determinant factors as \(\lambda\nu(c)\) times the determinant
of the stabilization-coordinate block.  Since the product is a nonzero
constant, \(\nu(c)\) is a unit, hence constant.
Coefficient comparison gives the two equations.  Conversely, they define
the required triangular automorphism without using any stabilization
variable.
\end{proof}

For squarefree \(A\), the complete data are therefore the affine
configuration of its roots, with represented/missing markings, and the
projective vector
\[
[B(\rho)]_{\rho\in Z(A)}
\]
modulo the finite permutations induced by affine symmetries.  Equivalently,
after compactifying the \(t\)-direction, the boundary tangent at a root
\(\rho\) is
\[
\tau_\rho=-\frac{3A'(\rho)}{B(\rho)},
\]
and the projective vector of these slopes is intrinsic.

\subsection{Consequences for the \texorpdfstring{\(q\)}{q}-family}

For
\[
A(c)=c(c+1),\qquad B_q(c)=qc^2-4c-2,
\]
the roots \(0\) and \(-1\) are respectively represented and missing, and
\[
B_q(0)=-2,\qquad B_q(-1)=q+2.
\]
Thus, for \(q\ne-2\),
\[
q=2\frac{\tau_0}{\tau_{-1}}-2.
\]
At \(q=-2\), the discriminant gains a plane factor and the residual
component is singular at \((u,v,c)=(0,0,-1)\); this separates the
exceptional member intrinsically.  These calculations give another proof
of the complete stable \(q\)-classification in the body.

\subsection{A three-dimensional degree-fifteen family}

Let
\[
s\in\C\setminus\{0,1\},\qquad d_1,d_2\in\C^*,
\]
and set
\[
A_s(c)=\frac{c(c+1)(c+s)}s.
\]
There is a unique cubic \(B_{s,d_1,d_2}\) satisfying
\[
B(0)=-2,\qquad B'(0)=-2A_s''(0),\qquad
B(-1)=d_1,\qquad B(-s)=d_2.
\]
Explicitly,
\[
B=-2-\frac{4(s+1)}s c+b_2c^2+b_3c^3,
\]
where
\[
b_2=
\frac{d_1s^3-d_2-2s^3-4s^2+4s+2}{s^2(s-1)},
\qquad
b_3=
\frac{d_1s^2-d_2-2s^2+2}{s^2(s-1)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Degree-fifteen stable moduli]
\label{thm:degree-fifteen-moduli}
The associated maps are polynomial Keller maps of component degrees
\((15,14,4)\), generic degree three, and generic monodromy \(S_3\).
Their stable equivalence relation is exactly
\[
(s,d_1,d_2)\sim(1/s,d_2,d_1).
\]
Consequently degree fifteen contains a three-dimensional stable-moduli
family, modulo this finite involution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The jet conditions at \(c=0\) are precisely the pole-cancellation
conditions in the cubic frame, so \cref{prop:general-frame} gives a Keller
map of generic degree three.  Since \(\deg A=\deg B=3\), the leading-term
formulas give component degrees \((15,14,4)\).

The inverse cubic is irreducible over the target function field because it
generates the degree-three source extension.  Its discriminant has valuation
one along the irreducible primitive discriminant divisor, so it is not a
square in that field.  A transitive subgroup of \(S_3\) arising from an
irreducible cubic is \(A_3\) or \(S_3\); the nonsquare discriminant excludes
\(A_3\).  Thus the generic monodromy is \(S_3\).

The represented root is \(0\), while the
two missing roots are \(-1,-s\).  By
\cref{thm:conductor-arrangement-classification}, an equivalence must fix the
represented root and either fix or interchange the missing roots.  This
gives the identity or the displayed involution.  The involution is realized
by
\[
(x,y,z)\longmapsto(x/s,sy,s^2z)
\]
and
\[
(a,b,c)\longmapsto(s^2a,sb,c/s).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Growing stable moduli]
\label{thm:growing-cubic-frame-moduli}
For every \(n\ge2\), the cubic frame contains a coarse stable-moduli locus
of dimension \(2n-3\), represented by generic-degree-three Keller maps of
ordinary degree \(4n+3\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take squarefree \(A\) of degree \(n\), with one represented root and
\(n-1\) missing roots.  Normalize the represented root to \(0\), impose
\[
A'(0)=1,\qquad B(0)=-2,\qquad B'(0)=-2A''(0),
\]
and prescribe arbitrary nonzero values of \(B\) at the other roots.
Hermite interpolation produces \(B\) of degree at most \(n\).
The affine root configuration contributes \(n-2\) parameters, and the
projective conductor-value vector contributes \(n-1\).  The classification
theorem identifies only a finite permutation quotient, giving
\((n-2)+(n-1)=2n-3\) moduli.  The leading terms have ordinary degree
\(4n+3\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Degree threshold inside the cubic frame]
\label{thm:cubic-frame-degree-threshold}
Every polynomial member of ordinary degree at most ten in the full cubic
frame is polynomially source--target equivalent to the degree-seven base
map.  Degree eleven is the first degree at which genuine moduli occur in
this frame.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(m=\deg A\) and \(n=\deg B\), their unique leading contributions have
degrees
\[
(4m+3,4m+2)\quad\text{and}\quad(4n+2,4n+1)
\]
in the first two coordinates.  Degree at most ten forces
\[
\deg A=1,\qquad\deg B\le2.
\]
After centering and scaling,
\[
A(C)=C,\qquad B(C)=-2+b_2C^2,
\]
because admissibility gives
\(B'(0)=-2A''(0)=0\).  The \(b_2\)-family is removed by the explicit
locally nilpotent flow already described in the classification of the
\(\alpha=0\) line.  The \(q\)-family supplies inequivalent degree-eleven
members.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Formal arcs and global separation}

For fixed \(q\), take
\[
A_s(c)=c+sc^2,\qquad
B_{s,q}(c)=-2-4sc+qs^2c^2.
\]
The special fiber \(s=0\) is the base map, and every \(s\ne0\) fiber lies in
the stable orbit indexed by \(q\).  The first three independently computed
coefficients of the formal source trivialization have degree triples
\[
(19,17,19),\quad(37,35,37),\quad(55,53,55).
\]
The first coefficient is independent of \(q\), and \(q\) first occurs in
the second.

\begin{proposition}[All-order formal-local versus stable separation]
\label{prop:formal-stable-separation}
For every \(q\), there is a coefficientwise formal source automorphism
\[
\Phi_s=\id+\sum_{n\ge1}s^nV_n,
\qquad V_n\in\C[x,y,z]^3,
\]
such that \(F_s\circ\Phi_s=F_0\) in \(\C[x,y,z][[s]]^3\).  Thus all
\(q\)-arcs are source-trivial to every formal order, while fibers with
nonzero complex \(s\) and different \(q\) remain pairwise stably
inequivalent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(F_s=F_0+sF_1+s^2F_2\), as checked directly from the frame.  Suppose
\(V_1,\ldots,V_{n-1}\) have been chosen so that the identity holds modulo
\(s^n\).  At order \(n\), the new coefficient occurs linearly and the
equation is
\[
DF_0\,V_n=-R_n,
\]
where \(R_n\) is a polynomial vector determined by the earlier
coefficients.  The determinant of \(DF_0\) is \(-2\), so its adjugate divided
by \(-2\) is a polynomial inverse.  Therefore
\[
V_n=-(DF_0)^{-1}R_n
\]
is polynomial, and induction constructs \(\Phi_s\) to all orders.  A map
congruent to the identity modulo
\(s\) has a continuous compositional inverse in the \(s\)-adic ring, so this
is a formal source automorphism.

Stable inequivalence of the nonzero complex fibers follows from the
intrinsic ratio of conductor values.  There is no contradiction: the
coefficientwise formal automorphism need not have bounded polynomial degree
in \((x,y,z)\), algebraize over \(\C[s]\), or specialize at \(s\ne0\).
\end{proof}
