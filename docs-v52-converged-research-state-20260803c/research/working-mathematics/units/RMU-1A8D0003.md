# Source splitting and the exact attained-value boundary

`RMU-1A8D0003` · `proposition` · statement version `1`

## Exact statement

For every generic-degree-three complex Keller normalization, base change to the affine source gives B tensor_R S isomorphic to S times C, where the S-factor is the canonical marked section and C is a normal quadratic S-algebra. After choosing a generator eta of its trace-zero summand, C is isomorphic to S[eta]/(eta^2-D). The generator and D are choice-dependent. The normalization is free over every attained target value, and Supp(Delta_F) is contained in the omitted-value locus, hence in the singular locus of the reduced nonproperness set.

## Hypotheses

- The setup and notation are those of RMU-1A8D0001.
- The omitted-values theorem RMU-C5C8680E is used for the final containment.

## Applies to

- Source base change and the location of a possible finite cubic flatness defect for arbitrary generic-degree-three Keller maps.

## Limitations

- The splitting has a canonical section but no canonical trace-zero generator or canonical displayed polynomial.
- Source splitting cannot be applied at a defect value because every such value is omitted.
- The proposition does not prove target flatness at omitted values.

## Arguments

### The source section splits the pulled-back normalization

`ARG-P1-SOURCE-SPLITTING` · `proof`

After base change to the source, the distinguished section is open and closed and leaves a rank-two normal algebra; the section is canonical although a quadratic generator is not.

Base change the finite normalization along the source map. The diagonal source section is both open and closed, so B tensor_R S splits canonically as S times a residual rank-two algebra C. Normality and factoriality of S identify C, after choosing a generator eta of its trace-zero line, with S[eta]/(eta^2-D). The factor S and its marked section are intrinsic, but eta and the displayed D depend on the trivialization. The product is finite flat over S. Faithfully flat descent at every attained target value therefore makes B flat there, proving that the defect support lies in the omitted-value set.

Does not establish:

- A canonical generator eta or canonical displayed polynomial D.
- Flatness at an omitted target value.
