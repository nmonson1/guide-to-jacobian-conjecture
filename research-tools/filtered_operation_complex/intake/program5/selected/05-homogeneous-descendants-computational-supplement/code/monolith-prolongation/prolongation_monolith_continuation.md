# Monoliths, prolongations, and the search for a dimension lower bound

## Status

This note continues the affine-Lie/Hankel calculation for the explicit
19-variable cubic-homogeneous counterexample.  It records one substantial
structural reduction, one exact boundary computation, and one negative result.

The main new theorem is:

> **Monolith–prolongation dichotomy.**  Let
> \(F=I+\mu(x,x,x)\) be a dimension-minimal noninjective
> cubic-homogeneous Keller map over an algebraically closed field of
> characteristic zero.  Let \(D\) be the minimal ideal containing a collision
> difference.  On \(D\), the Lie algebra generated by the partial
> multiplications is either
> \[
> \mathfrak{sl}(D)
> \qquad\text{or}\qquad
> \mathfrak{sp}(D,\omega).
> \]
> The symplectic case is possible only when \(\dim D\) is even.

Thus the vague problem of classifying arbitrary multiplication algebras is
reduced to two classical representation-theoretic extension problems.  The
explicit 19-variable example lies on the \(\mathfrak{sl}_{18}\) branch.

This does **not** yet prove a lower bound of 19.  In fact, the calculation also
shows why the raw affine-Lie and Hankel invariants cannot by themselves prove
stable minimality: the rank-sensitive suspension manufactures much of that
operator complexity while remaining stably left-right equivalent to the
smaller degree-three presentation.

All calculations specific to the displayed tensor are reproduced by
`verify_prolongation_structure.py` and
`verify_sl2_collision_obstruction.py`.

## 1. Correction to the earlier Capelli-density argument

The previous note said that after obtaining every trilinear map, “evaluating
one input at a constant gives all bilinear maps, and repeated composition gives
all arities.”  That sentence was too loose if “polynomial operation” is read as
an operation without parameters.

The required conclusion remains valid, with a cleaner proof that uses only odd
arities.

### Lemma 1.1 (odd-arity density)

Let \(A\) be a symmetric ternary algebra and let \(U\subseteq A\) contain all
products.  Suppose

\[
\mathcal M(A)=\operatorname{Hom}(A,U)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mu|_{U^3}\ne0.
\]

After allowing auxiliary variables to be specialized, the linear span of
ternary monomials contains every multilinear map

\[
A^{2j+1}\longrightarrow U
\qquad (j\ge0).
\]

#### Proof

Every rank-one unary map \(x\mapsto\alpha(x)u\), with \(u\in U\), is a linear
combination of unary multiplication contexts.  Choose
\(u_1,u_2,u_3\in U\) with
\(\mu(u_1,u_2,u_3)=u_0\ne0\).  Precompose the three slots by arbitrary
rank-one unary maps and postcompose by a unary context taking \(u_0\) to an
arbitrary \(v\in U\).  This realizes every rank-one trilinear tensor

\[
v\otimes\alpha_1\otimes\alpha_2\otimes\alpha_3.
\]

These span all trilinear maps.  If all multilinear maps of odd arity \(m\)
are available, insert such a map in one slot of \(\mu\), put rank-one unary
maps in the other two slots, and postcompose.  This realizes every rank-one
map of arity \(m+2\), completing the induction.  ∎

For the displayed algebra, take \(m=19\) and a nonzero element of
\(\operatorname{Hom}(\bigwedge^{19}A,U)\).  Its alternation is nonzero, so the
system of Capelli identities \(C_{19}\) fails.  Since \(\dim A=19\), the system
\(C_{20}\) holds automatically.

More generally, if \(D\) is an \(r\)-dimensional irreducible ideal on which the
unitalized multiplication algebra acts as \(\operatorname{End}(D)\), and
\(\mu(D,D,D)\ne0\), then the ambient algebra fails \(C_r\).  When \(r\) is
even, use an \((r+1)\)-linear map alternating in the first \(r\) variables and
regard the final input as an auxiliary variable.

## 2. The minimal collision algebra is monolithic

Let

\[
F(x)=x+\mu(x,x,x)
\]

be a cubic-homogeneous Keller map with a collision.  Write the two colliding
points as

\[
p=a+d,\qquad q=a-d,\qquad d\ne0.
\]

Expanding the collision gives

\[
\boxed{
 d+3\mu(a,a,d)+\mu(d,d,d)=0.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Assume from now on that the ambient dimension is minimal among
cubic-homogeneous collision counterexamples.

### Proposition 2.1

The associated ternary algebra may be assumed to satisfy:

1. \(A\) is generated by \(a,d\);
2. \(d\in A^3\) and \(\dim A/A^3\le1\);
3. every nonzero proper ideal contains \(d\);
4. consequently, either \(A\) is simple, or it has a unique minimal nonzero
   ideal \(D\), and \(d\in D\);
5. every proper quotient is Yagzhev, equivalently its associated map is
   invertible.

#### Proof

The subalgebra generated by \(a,d\) still contains the collision and inherits
the Engel condition, so minimality makes it all of \(A\).  Equation (2.1)
gives \(d\in A^3\).  Modulo \(A^3\), all ternary products vanish and only the
class of the generator \(a\) remains, so \(\dim A/A^3\le1\).

If \(I\) is a nonzero proper ideal not containing \(d\), the images of
\(p,q\) remain distinct and collide in \(A/I\), contradicting minimality.
Thus every nonzero proper ideal contains \(d\), and their intersection is the
unique minimal ideal.  ∎

The ideal \(D\) is irreducible under all partial multiplication operators:
an invariant subspace of \(D\) is exactly an ideal contained in \(D\).
After extending scalars to an algebraic closure, Burnside density gives

\[
\operatorname{Alg}^{1}\langle L_{x,y}|_D:x,y\in A\rangle
=\operatorname{End}(D).
\tag{2.2}
\]

## 3. A lower-dimensional nonhomogeneous counterexample lives in the collision fibre

Suppose first that \(D\ne A\), and choose a vector-space splitting

\[
A=B\oplus D,
\qquad B\simeq A/D.
\]

Because \(D\) is an ideal, the quotient component of \(F(b,u)\) is independent
of \(u\).  Hence

\[
F(b,u)=\bigl(\bar F(b),f_b(u)\bigr),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where \(f_b:D\to D\) has degree at most three.  The Jacobian is block
triangular, so

\[
1=\det JF=\det J\bar F(b)\,\det J_u f_b(u).
\]

The two factors are units in a polynomial ring and equal one at the origin;
therefore both are identically one.  The two collision points have the same
\(B\)-coordinate and give a collision of one fibre map \(f_{b_0}\).

### Proposition 3.1

For the minimal ideal \(D\),

\[
\boxed{\mu(D,D,D)\ne0.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

If \(D=A\), this is immediate because the map has a collision.  If
\(D\ne A\) and \(\mu(D,D,D)=0\), then the fibre map \(f_{b_0}\) in (3.1) has
degree at most two.  It is Keller and noninjective, contradicting Wang's
theorem that quadratic Keller maps are automorphisms.  ∎

Let

\[
r=\dim D,
\qquad
c=\dim\mu(D,D,D).
\]

The cubic part of \(f_{b_0}\) has output-mode rank \(c\).  Applying the
rank-sensitive suspension to this degree-at-most-three counterexample gives a
cubic-homogeneous counterexample in dimension \(r+c+1\).  If \(n=\dim A\) is
minimal, then

\[
\boxed{
 n\le r+c+1\le2r+1,
 \qquad
 r\ge\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is structural rather than an absolute lower bound, but it limits how
small the monolith of a minimal counterexample can be.

## 4. The multiplication Lie algebra on the monolith

Define

\[
\mathfrak g_D
=
\operatorname{Lie}
\langle L_{x,y}|_D:x,y\in A\rangle
\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(D).
\]

### Lemma 4.1

The representation of \(\mathfrak g_D\) on \(D\) is irreducible and

\[
\mathfrak g_D\subseteq\mathfrak{sl}(D).
\]

Moreover, \(\mathfrak g_D\) is semisimple.

#### Proof

Irreducibility follows from the minimality of \(D\).  The Keller/Engel
condition says that \(L_{x,x}\) is nilpotent for every \(x\), hence its trace
on \(D\) is zero.  Polarization,

\[
2L_{x,y}=L_{x+y,x+y}-L_{x,x}-L_{y,y},
\]

shows that every generator is traceless.

An irreducible finite-dimensional linear Lie algebra over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic zero is reductive and its radical acts by
scalars.  Here those scalars have trace zero, so the radical vanishes.  ∎

### Theorem 4.2 (monolith–prolongation dichotomy)

After extending scalars to an algebraic closure,

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak g_D=\mathfrak{sl}(D)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\mathfrak g_D=\mathfrak{sp}(D,\omega).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

The restricted tensor

\[
\mu_D:=\mu|_{\operatorname{Sym}^3D}
\]

is nonzero by Proposition 3.1.  For fixed \(u,v\in D\), the map

\[
w\longmapsto\mu_D(u,v,w)=L_{u,v}w
\]

lies in \(\mathfrak g_D\).  Thus, in the standard prolongation notation,

\[
0\ne\mu_D\in\mathfrak g_D^{(2)}
:=
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Sym}^3D,D)
\cap
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Sym}^2D,\mathfrak g_D).
\]

The Cartan–Kobayashi–Nagano classification says that an irreducible linear Lie
algebra with nonzero second prolongation is one of

\[
\mathfrak{gl}(D),\quad
\mathfrak{sl}(D),\quad
\mathfrak{sp}(D),\quad
\mathfrak{csp}(D).
\]

Lemma 4.1 makes the algebra semisimple and traceless, eliminating
\(\mathfrak{gl}\) and \(\mathfrak{csp}\).  ∎

This is the strongest general reduction obtained so far.  In odd monolith
dimension, only the \(\mathfrak{sl}\) branch remains.

## 5. Every minimal counterexample is an SL- or Sp-prolongation extension

Write \(B=A/D\) and choose a linear splitting.  Decompose the \(D\)-valued
part of the tensor according to the number of \(D\)-inputs:

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\tau_{3,0}&\in&\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Sym}^3D,D),\\
\tau_{2,1}&\in&\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Sym}^2D\otimes B,D),\\
\tau_{1,2}&\in&\operatorname{Hom}(D\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2B,D),\\
\tau_{0,3}&\in&\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Sym}^3B,D).
\end{array}
\]

Tensor symmetry and the definition of \(\mathfrak g_D\) give the sharper
placements

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{3,0}&\in\mathfrak g_D^{(2)},\\
\tau_{2,1}(\,\cdot,\,\cdot,b)&\in\mathfrak g_D^{(1)}&&\text{for each }b\in B,\\
\tau_{1,2}(\,\cdot,b_1,b_2)&\in\mathfrak g_D&&\text{for each }b_1,b_2\in B.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The quotient tensor \(\bar\mu:\operatorname{Sym}^3B\to B\) is Yagzhev because
\(A/D\) is a proper quotient.

### Codimension one

When \(B=ke\), the one-dimensional quotient has zero ternary product: its
map is \(s\mapsto s+\lambda s^3\), and the Keller condition forces
\(\lambda=0\).  Write a point as \(u+\tau e\), with \(u\in D\).  The
full nonlinear term is therefore \(D\)-valued and has the form

\[
H_D(u,\tau)
=
\mu_0(u,u,u)
+3\tau\mu_1(u,u)
+3\tau^2\mu_2(u)
+\tau^3\mu_3,
\tag{5.2}
\]

where

\[
\mu_0=\mu|_{D^3}\in\mathfrak g_D^{(2)},
\qquad
\mu_1=\mu(e,\,\cdot,\,\cdot)\in\mathfrak g_D^{(1)},
\qquad
\mu_2=\mu(e,e,\,\cdot)\in\mathfrak g_D,
\qquad
\mu_3=\mu(e,e,e)\in D.
\]

For every \(u,\tau\), the quadratic operator pencil

\[
L_{u+\tau e,u+\tau e}|_D
=
L^{(0)}_{u,u}+2\tau L^{(1)}_u+\tau^2L^{(2)}
\tag{5.3}
\]

must be nilpotent.  If \(D\ne A\), the collision midpoint cannot lie in \(D\), since then the
restriction to \(D\) would already be a smaller cubic-homogeneous
counterexample.  We may therefore choose \(e=a\), so the collision is
\(e+d,e-d\), and its equation becomes

\[
\boxed{
 d+3\mu_2(d)+\mu_0(d,d,d)=0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For \(\mathfrak g_D=\mathfrak{sl}(D)\), the first and second prolongations are
respectively divergence-free quadratic and cubic vector fields.  For
\(\mathfrak g_D=\mathfrak{sp}(D,\omega)\), the symplectic form identifies

\[
\mathfrak{sp}(D)^{(1)}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^3D^*,
\qquad
\mathfrak{sp}(D)^{(2)}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^4D^*.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Consequently, the search below dimension 19 can be organized into two
classical module problems rather than arbitrary cubic tensors.

## 6. Exact boundary calculation for the displayed 19-variable tensor

Let

\[
A=U\oplus kt,
\qquad \dim U=18,
\]

with

\[
U=\langle e_x,\ldots,e_{w_7}\rangle,
\qquad
W=\langle e_{w_1},\ldots,e_{w_7}\rangle.
\]

The exact tensor calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
A^3=U,
\qquad
U^3=W,
\qquad
\mu(W,U,U)=0.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus the restriction \(I+H|_U\) is the quasi-translation with inverse
\(I-H|_U\).  The noninvertibility is created by the mixed \(t\)-layers, not by
the internal algebra on \(U\).

In the decomposition (5.2), the exact dimensions are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{layer}&\text{linear operator span}&\text{Lie closure}&
\text{associative closure}\\ \hline
\mu_0=\mu|_{U^3}&20&20&20\\
\mu_1=\mu(t,U,U)&11&109&110\\
\mu_2=\mu(t,t,U)&1&1&1
\end{array}
\]

The operator \(\mu_2=L_{t,t}|_U\) has rank seven and square zero.  All three
layers together generate

\[
\operatorname{End}(U),\qquad \dim=324,
\]

associatively.

### A shorter conceptual certificate for \(\mathfrak{sl}_{18}\)

The associative closure \(M_{18}\) proves irreducibility.  Since
\(\mu_0\ne0\), the second prolongation is nonzero.  The classification in
Theorem 4.2 leaves only \(\mathfrak{sl}_{18}\) and \(\mathfrak{sp}_{18}\).

For the three collision Jacobians restricted to \(U\), solve

\[
A_i^TJ+JA_i=0,
\qquad J^T=-J.
\]

There are \(\binom{18}{2}=153\) unknown entries of \(J\).  The coefficient
matrix has rank 153 modulo \(p=1{,}000{,}003\), and a selected
\(153\times153\) determinant is

\[
\boxed{485934\not\equiv0\pmod{1{,}000{,}003}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Hence there is no nonzero invariant alternating form, ruling out the
symplectic branch.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak g_U=\mathfrak{sl}_{18}.}
\]

Together with the translation-column certificate from the previous note, this
recovers

\[
\mathfrak{sl}_{18}\ltimes k^{18}
\]

for the full 19-dimensional affine Lie envelope.

## 7. The raw affine-Lie and Hankel invariants are not stable invariants

At \(t=1\), the rank-sensitive fibre is

\[
K_f(X,w)=\bigl(X+Q(X)+Bw,\;w-q(X)\bigr).
\]

The triangular automorphisms

\[
S(X,w)=(X,w-q(X)),
\qquad
T(Y,v)=(Y+Bv,v)
\]

satisfy exactly

\[
\boxed{K_f=T\circ(K\times\operatorname{id}_7)\circ S.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The verifier checks (7.1) symbolically.  Yet the suspended homogeneous tensor
has multiplication algebra \(\operatorname{Hom}(k^{19},U)\), while the three
nonlinear branch Jacobians in the displayed 11-dimensional presentation have
only

\[
\dim\operatorname{Lie}=29,
\qquad
\dim\operatorname{Alg}=30.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Equation (7.2) is only a diagnostic—the 11-dimensional map has quadratic as
well as cubic terms—but it makes the main point concrete: the suspension can
manufacture a large affine-Lie/Hankel realization while preserving the stable
cover.  Therefore a global lower bound needs either:

1. a theorem applying to **every** minimal collision algebra, such as the
   prolongation extension theorem sought below; or
2. a genuinely filtered/Rees invariant that survives triangular stable
   equivalence.

The raw \(M_{18}\), \(\mathfrak{sa}_{18}\), and 19-state Hankel rank do not yet
have that invariance.

## 8. A uniform obstruction to the principal \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) collision pencil

Let

\[
V=\operatorname{Sym}^m(k^2)
\]

with basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_m\), and standard \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-operators

\[
Ee_i=i e_{i-1},
\qquad
Fe_i=(m-i)e_{i+1},
\qquad
He_i=(m-2i)e_i.
\]

The principal regular nilpotent conic is

\[
M(s,t)=s^2E+stH-t^2F.
\]

The tensor-symmetry identities of a collision pencil would require

\[
Hu=2Ev,
\qquad
Hv=-2Fu.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Write \(u=\sum u_i e_i\), \(v=\sum v_i e_i\).  For \(1\le i\le m\), the two
coefficient equations are

\[
(m-2i+2)u_{i-1}=2iv_i,
\]

\[
(m-2i)v_i=-2(m-i+1)u_{i-1}.
\]

Elimination gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=
\bigl[(m-2i)(m-2i+2)+4i(m-i+1)\bigr]u_{i-1}\\
&=m(m+2)u_{i-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In characteristic zero, all \(u_i\) vanish, and then all \(v_i\) vanish.
Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The principal regular nilpotent conic is collision-incompatible in every
 dimension.}
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This does not eliminate regular nilpotent pencils—the paper's five-dimensional
family is a non-principal deformation—but it identifies exactly where the
moduli problem begins.  Any regular collision pencil must leave the maximally
symmetric \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) orbit.

## 9. The revised route to a bound above 16

The prolongation theorem suggests the following single target.

### Extension theorem target \(E(N)\)

For every \(r+q\le N\), let \(D\) be the standard module for
\(\mathfrak{sl}_r\) or \(\mathfrak{sp}_r\), and let \(B\) be a one-generated
Yagzhev ternary algebra of dimension \(q\).  No extension tensor of the form
(5.1) simultaneously satisfies:

1. all Engel trace identities;
2. irreducibility of the action on \(D\);
3. the collision equation (2.1);
4. minimality of the monolith \(D\).

Then \(E(16)\) proves that the minimum cubic-homogeneous counterexample
dimension is at least 17.  The statement \(E(18)\) proves the sharp lower bound
19.

This is still difficult, but it is much smaller than the original search:
there are only two Lie types, and every mixed tensor belongs to an explicit
prolongation module.

### Concrete computational program

1. **Start with codimension one.**  For each \(r\le17\), use (5.2)--(5.4).
   Decompose the Engel equations into irreducible \(\mathrm{SL}_r\)- or
   \(\mathrm{Sp}_r\)-modules rather than expanding coordinate tensors.
2. **Eliminate the symplectic branch first.**  Replace the tensors by a quartic,
   cubic, and quadratic Hamiltonian package using (5.5).  Test whether the
   Engel nullcone plus (5.4) forces a common isotropic flag, contradicting
   irreducibility.
3. **For the special-linear branch, use the trace-free polynomial-vector-field
   complex.**  The equations live in the kernels of divergence maps, giving
   small Schur-functor blocks after polarization.
4. **Use the principal-\(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) calculation as the closed-orbit
   boundary condition.**  Any surviving component must be a deformation away
   from the principal conic; compute its normal obstruction before solving the
   full global equations.
5. **Search for a universal alternating consequence.**  The ideal output is a
   Capelli-type identity showing that a collision extension with
   \(r+q\le16\) would have to fail \(C_{17}\), impossible in that dimension.
6. **Only after the representation reduction, use modular Gröbner bases.**
   The certificate should be a finite list of highest-weight equations and a
   modular nonzero minor or Nullstellensatz identity.

The most promising immediate theorem is the codimension-one symplectic
exclusion.  It has the smallest modules and a Hamiltonian reformulation.  The
special-linear branch appears to contain the displayed boundary example and
is likely the harder one.
